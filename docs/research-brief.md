# Research discussion brief

Working question: can a reusable cross-domain review skill improve the actionability of creative decisions while preserving the creator's goal, avoiding unsupported agreement/disagreement, and updating appropriately after new evidence?

Artifact: an alpha skill, structured report helper, fictional cross-domain scenarios, three-condition comparison prompts and a versioned evaluation harness. The software can support a discussion with a prospective supervisor; no collaboration, ethics approval, publication acceptance or empirical benefit is established.

Potential contribution to investigate: a task framing and evaluation that distinguishes useful challenge from blanket criticism and separates factual stability under attitude changes from legitimate revision under new evidence. A stronger ordinary prompt is included as a baseline, so any observed benefit cannot simply be attributed to comparing detailed instructions against an intentionally weak request.

The planned formal set contains 24 authored projects across four domains. A full repeated-condition design generates 432 responses; a reduced prespecified design generates 180. Independent project-level analysis and reviewer provenance are necessary. Native skill discovery, live source retrieval, audiovisual inspection, actual player experience and long-term market outcomes are outside the text-only experiment.

Open methodological issues for discussion: ecological validity of fictional briefs, case authoring bias, model selection, budget matching, imperfect reviewer blinding, calibration of evidence-update judgments, number and expertise of independent reviewers, missing outcomes, and institutional requirements for creator trials.

Materials for a discussion: [protocol](../evaluations/PROTOCOL.md), [rubric](../evaluations/rubric.md), [related work](related-work.md), [architecture](architecture.md), [validation status](validation.md), and [task progress](task-progress.md). The related-work comparison is preliminary and does not establish novelty. A full literature review and preregistered analysis would be future work if a formal research route is chosen.

No mentor has been contacted. An eventual message should ask for methodological feedback and disclose the alpha's actual evidence, rather than promise publishable results. The project owner should decide recipients and authorize any outreach.
