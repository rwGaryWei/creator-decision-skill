# Product scope and decisions

Creator Decision helps an AI-assisted creator choose their next investment of effort. Version 0.1 covers four domains—web/tools, video, short drama and games—and three stages: idea, prototype, feedback. The original purpose is a helpful review and improvement partner that challenges assumptions without dismissing creative intent.

The short decision card is the primary interface. Evidence records and scripts support it; they are not an obligatory questionnaire. A worthwhile outcome may be a clearer artistic intention, a smaller uncertainty, a repaired experience, a justified decision to continue, or a rational stopping point.

Included: selective clarification; domain-sensitive inspection; source/observation boundaries; strengths and obstacles; concrete alternative improvements; AI production tradeoffs; one bounded experiment; revision after new evidence; an explicit human decision record when requested.

For work intended for other people, audience/market acceptance is an explicit part of the macro assessment: target situation, need or desired experience, alternatives, reasons to choose or reject, discovery constraints and evidence gaps. This applies without a revenue goal; use, viewing, play and repeat engagement are distinct from payment. The prohibition on unsupported forecasts does not exclude reasoned market analysis. A personal-only project can be reviewed against its stated goal without imposing a commercial test.

Excluded from this release: automatic market forecasts, success/virality probabilities, AI detectors, automated copyright clearance, scraping infrastructure, generated end products, a web SaaS, automatic outreach or purchases, and claims of independent usefulness without testing. Steam can be an optional game source; no Steam ID is required for any flow.

The implementation must retain creative goals such as quiet pacing, unconventional aesthetics, play, learning and expression. It must not silently substitute revenue or broad popularity. A user's confidence is context, not new factual evidence. Significant changes to protected creative intent are choices for that creator.

Owner-confirmed principle: investigate comparable works online when a design appears to conflict with the goal, using similar themes, pacing or experiences and explaining consequential differences. Consider the relevant factors together; make strengths, weaknesses and unresolved questions clear. The Skill supplies concrete design options but does not choose on the author's behalf. Its macro assessment precedes detailed options. Missing access, audience evidence or resource information remains explicitly unknown.

Product scope and the advisory role have been reviewed by the owner. Actual usefulness still needs review. MIT has been selected and the 36-run exploratory pilot completed; the current route is product improvement and internal owner review before formal research. Formal-run settings, independent reviewers and next-version investment remain future decisions. No external participant has been contacted and no research approval is represented as granted.

Release gates are project proposals, not empirical industry standards. Alpha can be public with truthful limits. Calling the product a validated v1 requires no unresolved critical failures, completed behavioral review, and independent creator use. A proposed practical trial is four creators spanning the four domains, with at least three completing their chosen review without intervention; this would still be an early usability signal, not market validation.
