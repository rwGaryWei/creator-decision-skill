# Video

Identify the viewing situation, audience motivation, opening promise, length/format, intended information or emotion, and desired payoff. “Daily AI videos” describes production frequency, not a reason to watch.

With a script, inspect information order, causal explanation, unnecessary detours, and whether the payoff fulfills the promise. With actual accessible footage/audio, cite inspected timestamps for pacing, legibility, continuity, voice delivery, sound balance, and audiovisual alignment. Do not infer those properties from a transcript or a few frames.

Distinguish the reason to click, the reason to keep watching, understanding/feeling during the piece, and the reason to return. Neither a striking generated image nor a misleading hook guarantees satisfaction. For an experimental art video, ambiguity may be deliberate; assess against its intended experience rather than automatically simplifying it.

Offer changes with consequences: changing the opening may clarify value but reduce mystery; using one visual motif may improve coherence but reduce spectacle; lowering frequency may permit factual review. Preserve what the creator considers essential.

Example experiment: compare two openings with the same topic and body. Ask intended viewers to state the promise before and the main explanation after viewing, recording confusion at specific moments. If participants are unavailable, first check script claims against primary sources and compare the promise with the final explanation. Do not label this audience validation.

AI tradeoffs: factual review, visual continuity, generated text in frames, pronunciation, rights/provenance, consistency, and revision workload. Verify platform rules when actually relevant; do not invent universal disclosure or ranking rules.

Feedback: record where viewers came from and which version they saw. Retention, clicks, comments, and self-reported liking answer different questions; public comments rarely expose the full audience denominator.
