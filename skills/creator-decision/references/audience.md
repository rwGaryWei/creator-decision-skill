# Audience and market acceptance

Assess whether the intended people have a reason to choose the proposed work. This is distinct from whether it can be built or whether its internal rules make sense. An uncertain answer must still explain the acceptance case, obstacles and evidence gaps. Do not use “cannot predict success” as a substitute for analysis.

## Frame the relevant outcome

Use the creator's goal. For a tool, distinguish trying it, completing a task, returning and paying. For video or drama, distinguish choosing to watch, understanding or feeling the intended payoff, finishing, returning and paying where relevant. For a game, distinguish interest, actual play, continued engagement and purchase. None automatically establishes the next. Do not make payment the default for every work or override an explicitly personal learning/expression goal.

## Assess the choice, not just the artifact

- Specify the candidate audience and situation, not simply “students” or “everyone.” Label an unconfirmed segment as a hypothesis.
- Explain the recurring problem or desired experience, its importance, and the current workaround or competing use of time. Novelty and AI-assisted production alone do not demonstrate demand.
- State why someone might choose this work over existing products, other content, manual methods or doing nothing. Examine setup, switching, trust, attention and price costs as relevant.
- Identify a plausible way the intended audience could discover it, with any access or distribution gaps. Do not invent platform reach, ranking rules, recruitment access or customer-acquisition figures.
- Show the strongest supporting and opposing evidence and the decisive unknown. Where commercial viability is a goal, distinguish willingness to pay from actual payment and consider delivery/maintenance costs without inventing conversion rates or sales forecasts.

Use [evidence guidance](evidence.md) for live research. Comparable-product pages establish reported features, not satisfied demand for this project. Seek relevant audience accounts or behavior as well; record sampling and differences. Separate creator self-promotion from independent feedback. A few search-selected comments are leads, not representative research. Do not infer an unserved market from a competitor feature you did not find, or universal rejection from one dissatisfied user.

## Turn uncertainty into a useful next check

Give a scoped conclusion such as “a plausible segment, but no demonstrated switching advantage,” explain its basis and offer the author's options. Connect the proposed minimum version to the largest acceptance uncertainty; a cheap artifact that cannot test that uncertainty is only a feasibility demonstration.

Name the observation that would strengthen or weaken the case. For example, compare a target user's current method with a prototype including input and correction effort, then check voluntary reuse on a later relevant occasion. Use domain-appropriate viewing or play behavior for creative work. Declare interpretation rules before results; any numerical threshold is a project choice, not an industry guarantee.

If target people are unavailable, do feasible desk research and internal checks now, state the remaining external test and leave audience validation open. No fabricated participants, simulated customer validation or automatic outreach. Keep the final decision with the creator, including choosing a learning project despite weak adoption evidence.
