# Platform choice and discovery

Quality, audience fit and access to an audience are different questions. Help the creator understand all three when publishing or adoption matters. Promotion may be the current bottleneck, but do not declare it universally more important than the work or assume paid reach is necessary.

## Choose a route grounded in the creator's situation

Use the stated platform, language, target audience, format, account history, existing channels, time and budget. Keep unavailable account analytics unknown. If no platform has been selected, compare a small relevant shortlist by audience fit, discovery routes, format/production effort, publishing access, conversion path and measurement limits; ask only for a missing choice that materially changes the plan. A named example is not a confirmed selection.

Read current official sources before asserting platform features, AI-content requirements, promotion eligibility, prices or distribution rules. Distinguish Douyin from TikTok and local/account-specific offerings. Do not repeat unsupported rules such as guaranteed first-post exposure, a fixed traffic pool, “new accounts must pay,” or paid boosting suppressing later organic reach. Explain what cannot be verified.

## Produce a concrete discovery plan

Specify the intended audience, entry point, publishable asset, reason to notice it, next action and how the creator can observe that action. Adapt the plan to the medium: a tool needs a useful demo and a path to trying the task; video/drama needs an honest promise and a payoff; a game needs an understandable play experience and a feasible route to a demo or store page.

Compare appropriate routes rather than prescribe all of them:

- Organic platform discovery: propose a specific title/cover/opening or demo and one focused variable to inspect. Explain that actual exposure is uncertain; do not promise algorithmic distribution.
- Existing audiences, search, relevant communities or collaborations: state whether access actually exists and the effort involved. No audience or collaborator is presumed available. Prepare material when useful; do not post, contact or spam people without authorization.
- Optional paid promotion: explain the chosen objective, eligible asset, audience settings actually available, creator-approved spending ceiling, observation period and stopping conditions. If budget is unknown, present the route conditionally without inventing an amount or initiating payment. Verify the live offering before any order; no automatic renewal or repeated spending by inference.

Make the smallest visibility experiment test a specific uncertainty. Define its asset/version, route, time and money limits, expected observations and what to do if evidence is inconclusive. Reach planning is part of making the work testable, not a reason to demand a full marketing campaign for every draft.

## Interpret observations without confusing them

Where available, distinguish exposure → meaningful viewing/visit → intended benefit or task completion → voluntary return/follow → purchase. A follow is not observed repeat engagement. Define denominators and observation windows; missing analytics are unknown, not zero. A popularity objective may legitimately prioritize reach, but reach alone does not establish trust, satisfaction or learning.

Low exposure leaves audience acceptance inconclusive. Exposure with weak response suggests examining targeting, promise, execution or measurement before blaming the entire idea. More paid views without downstream benefit does not establish product-market fit. Record organic and paid sources separately where the platform permits; do not infer causal uplift from an uncontrolled before/after comparison, since audience, timing and distribution can change. Count production and promotion effort as well as spend. When the agreed budget/window ends, review the evidence rather than automatically top up.
