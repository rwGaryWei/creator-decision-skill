The brief points to two possible barriers: reading the agenda and understanding which sessions can be attended together. Both could prevent attendees from finding relevant sessions. Because the notes are synthetic and no interface is provided, they are hypotheses to investigate—not evidence that either problem exists or that one matters more.

Keep the first revision narrow:

- Use larger, resizable text with enough space for session titles, descriptions, and times.
- Show each session’s start and end time consistently. For sessions an attendee selects, add an explicit “Overlaps with…” message; don’t rely on color alone.
- Keep topic and relevance information easy to scan so conflict information supports the central task.

Then ask a few representative attendees, including people who use enlarged text or assistive technology, to find two relevant sessions they can attend in full and explain their choices. Observe whether they can read the content, understand its relevance, and spot overlaps without help. Test the current version alongside the revision if one exists.

Use those observations to choose the next investment: persistent reading difficulties call for more accessibility work; missed conflicts call for clearer scheduling; difficulty choosing relevant sessions calls for better descriptions or discovery. Avoid committing to a broad redesign until you have that evidence.
